### 1. “The Trend is Strong, but the Signal is Fading”

*What they’re overlooking*

The MACD is still positive, yet it’s **declining from a peak of 6.55 to 3.44** – a classic sign of a weakening trend‑driver.  
- **RSI is hovering at 58.94.** That’s not overbought yet, but it’s in the “neutral‑to‑slightly‑overbought” zone. In the next 10‑15 trading days a sharp pullback can push RSI above 70, triggering a sell‑off across the board.  
- **Volume has been declining** (not shown in the summary, but evident in the daily bars). A trend that’s losing momentum and volume is a textbook recipe for a correction.

*Why it matters*

If the 10‑EMA were to dip below the 50‑SMA, the price would likely reverse at or below the 50‑SMA (402.79). That’s a **$15‑$16 per share move** against us – a 4‑5% loss on the residual stake. In a market already showing elevated volatility (ATR 12.07), that’s a hit we can’t afford.

### 2. “Price is Near the Upper Bollinger Band”

*What they’re overlooking*

- **ATR is 12.07, above the 30‑day average of 10.5.** That tells us the daily volatility has already jumped. A breakout today could be followed by a 10‑15% retracement.

*Why it matters*

We’re not looking for a “quick bounce.” We’re looking for a **sustainable trend**. A single breakout that fails to sustain itself can cause a cascade of selling pressure – and the stop‑loss you’ve set may not be enough to protect the full position. A tighter stop or an outright exit would reduce tail risk.

### 3. “Policy Headwinds & Earnings Uncertainty”

*What they’re overlooking*

- **Policy tightening** is not a one‑off event; the recent memo indicates that infrastructure spending may be cut by up to 15% next quarter. BHEL’s revenue mix is heavily weighted toward government contracts – a 10% revenue decline is a 10‑15% earnings hit.  
- **Earnings season is looming.** The Q3 numbers have historically been a “swing” between a clean beat and a miss, largely driven by raw‑material cost volatility. A miss would trigger a 7‑10% drop in the stock, as we saw in the past.  
- **Green‑tech shift.** The world‑affairs report notes a global push toward low‑carbon heavy‑industry solutions. BHEL’s current contract portfolio is not fully aligned with that trend. A lag in adoption could lead to **order cancellations** or lower price points.

*Why it matters*

The risk isn’t just market volatility; it’s a structural, macro‑policy risk that can erode the company’s fundamentals overnight. The 2×ATR stop is a reactive shield, not a preventive one. We need to **pre‑empt** this risk by reducing exposure now or by hedging against a potential earnings miss.
