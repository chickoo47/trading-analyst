### Takeaway

## 3. A Moderately Balanced Approach

### Position sizing
- **Trim 20 % of the current holding** – this is less aggressive than the 30–40 % but still removes a chunk of exposure.
- **Use a 1.5×ATR stop** (~₹18) on the trimmed portion. That gives a tighter cushion (≈4‑5 %) but still protects against a sudden swing. The remaining 80 % stays in place with a **trailing stop** that follows the 10‑EMA or 50‑SMA at a 1×ATR cushion. This way, if the trend keeps going, you capture gains; if it turns, you exit without a big tail.

### Hedge / optional add‑on
- **Buy a protective put** with a strike near the current price (e.g., ₹410). This caps downside to roughly ₹20‑30 % of the trimmed portion without fully liquidating. You pay a premium, but you’re protecting the residual stake and keeping the upside potential intact.

### Re‑entry logic
- **If the price dips below the 50‑SMA** (≈₹403) or the 10‑EMA (≈₹414), consider a **dollar‑cost averaging** entry with a tighter 1×ATR stop. That gives you a clearer “buy” signal in a defined range.

### Why this works
- **Capital preservation** – You’re not exposing 100 % to the potential volatility spike. A 20 % trim is a modest “cushion” that protects a core of the position.
- **Upside capture** – By keeping the bulk of the position, you stay in the game if the stock continues to rally. If it breaks above the Bollinger upper band, you’ll have the momentum to ride it further.
- **Risk control** – A 1.5×ATR stop on the trimmed part is tight enough to prevent a 10‑20 % crash but not so tight that it triggers on normal intraday noise. The trailing stop on the remaining portion ensures you’re not stuck if the trend reverses.
- **Strategic flexibility** – The protective put is a safety net that does not force a full exit, and the re‑entry plan gives you a systematic way to add back if the stock dips into a better value zone.

## Bottom line

- **Aggressive sell** protects against a sudden downside but may be premature and locks in a partial exit before the trend could fully materialize.
- **Conservative hold** keeps upside but exposes you to the same volatility that could erase the gains in the short term.
- A **moderate trim + tighter stop + optional hedge** gives you a balanced risk‑return profile: you reduce the worst‑case scenario while still sitting on a bullish trend that could deliver a 10‑15 % run if the breakout happens.

The market is not binary; it’s a spectrum of probabilities. By trimming a bit, tightening stops, and leaving room to buy back on a pullback, you hedge the downside without surrendering the upside potential. That’s the neutral‑risk approach I recommend.
